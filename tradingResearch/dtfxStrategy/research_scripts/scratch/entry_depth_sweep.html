Two shapes, one trade-off

Profit factor is U-shaped — best at the shallowest entries, worst around 60%, recovering (for a different reason — win rate, not R:R) toward 90%. Trade frequency only ever goes one way: up, roughly 450× from the shallowest to the deepest entry tested.

Reading the shape

Why profit factor falls, then rises. From 10% to ~60%, moving the entry deeper shrinks the reward (target is now closer) while win rate barely moves at first — pure R:R decay. Past 60%, a different effect takes over: the entry is now so deep into the zone that only a strong, decisive move reaches it at all, and win rate climbs sharply (50.6%→74.2% from 10% to 90%) enough to drag profit factor back up even as R:R keeps falling. Two different edges, not one continuous effect.

Why Sharpe looks worst exactly where profit factor looks best. At a 10% entry there are only 268 trades a year — about one every 1.4 days. A daily-return Sharpe calculation sees mostly flat, no-trade days punctuated by rare, huge jumps; that's a real property of trading this infrequently, but it makes the daily Sharpe number a poor fit for comparing across a 450× range of trade frequency. Expected R and profit factor are the more honest read of quality at the shallow end; Sharpe becomes more meaningful once trading frequency is high enough to actually fill most days (roughly 20%+ entry, where daily Sharpe holds fairly steady around 3–4 regardless of depth).

Same architecture, same caveats as the ranking file: modeled 1.6-pip round-trip spread (confirmed sufficient for this backtest — 0.8 pips each way on market-order entries and exits), pre-v26 concurrency approximation, $2M position-sizing cap (which is most of why excess kurtosis on the daily equity curve runs into the hundreds-to-thousands at several depths — a fixed-dollar-per-trade cap measured as a percentage return necessarily decays as equity grows past it; not evidence of a large real single-trade risk).
Every row passed a three-independent-thirds robustness check (profitable in each third of the 17-year history, not just on aggregate) at the time it was tested in this research thread — see goal80_entry_variants_research.py and goal81_shallow_entry_two_account.py for the full derivation, including the population-selection confound that had to be ruled out before trusting the shallow-entry numbers at all.
