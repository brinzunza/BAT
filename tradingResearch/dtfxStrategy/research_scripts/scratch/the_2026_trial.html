The 2026 Trial

The two-account setup from The Risk Ceiling — $100 buy-only + $100 sell-only, one confirmed open position per account, 1.6-pip spread on every entry — started completely fresh on 2026-01-01 instead of carrying 23 years of prior compounding, at 3% risk, compared across minimum zone heights.

Read the dollar figures for 5 and 10 pips with real skepticism

At 5 and 10 pips, this system still trades often enough (16-101 times/day) and wins often enough to run into the same $2M-position-sizing-cap compounding effect flagged in Head to Head — the "$166 million" and "$18.6 million" final balances below are the sizing model compounding unrealistically fast, not a credible 8-month forecast. Read win rate, trades/day, and the robustness section as the real signal; read 20 and 30 pips' final balances (still small enough that the cap barely matters) as the more believable dollar figures.

The finding that matters most on this page

At both 5 and 10 pips, win rate falls in every successive third of 2026 — 83.6% → 76.7% → 64.8%, and 75.7% → 68.0% → 59.6%. That's not noise around a flat average, it's a trend, and it points the same direction as an unresolved question from earlier research: the full-history backtest showed win rate jump sharply starting in 2021 (~81% to ~94.5%) with no confirmed explanation. If that jump was ever partly a data artifact rather than a real, permanent regime, 2026's declining numbers are exactly what unwinding it would look like — and every parameter on this page (minimum zone height, risk, all of it) was chosen using data from inside that same unexplained period.

20 pips is the one size that doesn't show this decay (65.8% → 62.0% → 70.1%, roughly flat, if anything ticking up) — worth weighing against its much lower trade count (107-193 per third) before reading too much into that stability.

Is this ready for live trading?

No — not yet.

This is a genuinely more rigorous backtest than where this research started — a real overshoot-gated entry, a validated target, a minimum-zone-size floor, risk sized against an actual margin-checked two-account model, and now a fresh-start, robustness-checked 2026 trial. But rigor within a backtest is not the same thing as readiness for real capital, and several specific gaps stand between here and live.

✕
Every parameter was chosen on the same data being used to validate it
Minimum zone height and risk-per-trade were both selected by testing directly against this same 2003/2009/2018–2026 dataset, with no reserved holdout period that was never touched during tuning. That's the textbook setup for overfitting to have crept in somewhere, even unintentionally.
✕
The 2021+ win-rate jump is still unexplained
Flagged in dtfx.md, never resolved — and this exact 2026 trial sits entirely inside that unexplained period, with its own declining-WR pattern raising the same question again from a new angle.
~
Execution is modeled only as a fixed 1.6-pip spread
No slippage beyond spread, no latency, no partial fills, no requotes, no weekend-gap handling beyond whatever the 1-minute bars themselves contain. Real fills will be worse than this on at least some trades.
~
The concurrency model is a known simplification
One confirmed slot per side across all four pairs combined, per goal79_two_account_portfolio.py's own docstring — this project has a more accurate cross-symbol resting-order race simulator elsewhere that wasn't used here.
✕
No paper-trading or forward-test track record exists
Every result on this page and every linked page is a backtest against historical data. This exact mechanism has not been run against a live or simulated real-time feed even once.
✕
No account-level risk controls beyond position sizing
No daily loss limit, no circuit breaker, no anomaly detection for a broken feed or a stuck connection — the live bot this project actually runs (PRODLIVETRADING) trades a different strategy entirely; this mechanism has never been wired into any live risk infrastructure.

What would change the answer: get an actual explanation for the 2021+ shift (a feed/vendor check, not another backtest); pick a true holdout window now — 2026 onward, say — and freeze every parameter before that window starts, so nothing downstream ever gets tuned against it; paper-trade the frozen configuration for a real stretch of live data; model realistic execution; and re-run this exact analysis on the production concurrency simulator instead of the simplified one. Until then, treat every number on this page as research, not a plan.
