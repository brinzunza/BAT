Reading this against the other view

This is the mirror image of The 2026 Scorecard's continuation view. Both are honest, both are "correct" — they just answer different questions. The Scorecard asks "if this account had really been running since 2009, how did 2026 look?" (answer: enormous in dollars, tiny in percent, because $2M-cap saturation flattens the percentage math). This page asks "if I opened these two accounts today with $100 each, what would I be looking at?" — and here, nowhere near the cap yet, the compounding is the whole story: $200 becomes $95,290 in eight months at the 30% entry depth.

2,579 trades in 8 months is a small sample relative to the 131,369-trade full-history backtest this rule is validated on — treat this specific run as one draw from that same distribution, not a new, independent result. The trade-level edge (WR 50.91%, PF 2.489) is consistent with the historical average; the exact dollar path is not something to expect to repeat.
Same modeling throughout: 1.6-pip round-trip spread, pre-v26 concurrency approximation, no slippage or live verification.
