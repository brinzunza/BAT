Why the percentages look small

These returns are measured as a continuation of the full 2009–2026 simulation — the same account that's been compounding for 17 years, not a fresh reset. By the time 2026 opens, combined equity is already $2.73B (30% pick) or $2.33B (v1), deep past the $2M position-sizing cap that's been in place since about ten days into the 2009 backtest. Past that cap, every trade risks a fixed dollar amount (5% of $2,000,000 = $100,000) regardless of how large the account has grown — so a trade that would have moved the needle by several percent back in 2010 now moves it by a few thousandths of a percent, because the base it's measured against is a thousand times larger.

That's exactly why the dollar growth still matters even though the percentages look tame: the 30% pick added +$85.6M in these eight-plus months, the 50% baseline added +$61.4M — real, continued compounding, just no longer visible as a dramatic percentage against an already-enormous base.

Win rate and profit factor in 2026 (46.99% / 2.146 for the 30% pick, 48.16% / 1.162 for v1) track closely with each strategy's full 17-year average — the edge reads as intact, not as fresh out-of-sample decay or a lucky stretch. Same caveats as everywhere else in this research: modeled 1.6-pip round-trip spread, pre-v26 concurrency approximation, no live verification.
