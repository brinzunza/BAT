The Risk Ceiling

Two questions that sound like the same question: "how much should I risk per trade?" Tested variant A (fixed 1:1R, the profitable target from Head to Head) under this project's actual live architecture — two separate $100 sub-accounts, buy-only and sell-only, never pooled, one confirmed open position per side, 50x leverage, 1.6-pip spread on every entry — sweeping both minimum zone height and risk per trade. The two questions below give genuinely different answers, and mixing them up is the mistake to avoid.

"How fast can I reach $1M, and how risky is that race?"

0.00% ruin

Block-bootstrap Monte Carlo, 1,000 paths per risk level, 1%-15% risk, 5- and 10-pip zone floors: every single path reaches $1M, typically in under a year. Essentially safe at any risk tested — because the race is over long before a real losing streak has time to occur.

"What if I keep compounding at that risk, indefinitely?"

Ruin above ~7-10%

A deterministic, single-pass replay of the full 23.7-year trade history (not bootstrapped) shows a sharp cliff: spectacular growth at 5-7% risk, then near-total ruin one or two risk steps higher — because a strategy trading hundreds of thousands of times across decades eventually meets a losing streak big enough to matter.

Ruin needs enough trades in a row to matter, at a large enough account size to matter. The $1M finish line arrives so quickly here (months, not years) that neither condition has time to develop — this table is answering "is the sprint to $1M safe," not "is this risk level safe forever," and the two questions have different answers for exactly the reason the cliff chart above shows.

The recommendation

For a bounded goal — grow $200 to $1M, then stop or re-evaluate

3-5% risk on a 5-10 pip minimum zone height is well-supported: fast (typically under a year per the Monte Carlo), zero measured ruin risk across 1,000 resampled paths, and drawdowns along the way stay in the 8-16% range rather than the 85%+ seen over a full 23-year compounding run.

For sustained, indefinite compounding at a fixed risk fraction

Treat 5% as already close to the edge — it produced an 85-96% historical drawdown even where it didn't fully ruin, and the very next risk step tested (10%) did ruin. Prefer 1-3% risk if the actual plan is to keep re-risking the same fraction for years rather than scaling risk down as equity grows past the point a $1M target would normally prompt a re-think.

Across both the earlier minimum-zone-size sweep and this risk analysis together, a 10+ pip minimum zone height paired with 3-5% risk is the best-supported combination this project has tested so far for variant A.

What this is (and isn't)

The deterministic "cliff" replay uses one specific historical ordering of trades. A different history could have put that losing streak somewhere else in the sequence, or nowhere at all in 23 years — the point isn't that ruin happens at exactly this risk level, it's that a cliff exists somewhere near this level for this trade distribution, and it's much closer to "safe-looking" risk levels than intuition suggests.
The Monte Carlo caps each path at $1M or 50,000 trades. It is a correct, honest answer to "how risky is the race to $1M" — it is not evidence that any tested risk level is safe to run forever, precisely because it never runs a path that long.
