Head to Head

Both variants share the exact same entry: every swing is a zone, price has to leave it behind and swing back to tag the exact 50% midpoint before an entry counts (Reading the Tape). They only differ on exit — (A) takes a fixed 1:1R at the zone's own far edge; (C) targets the last confirmed higher-high / lower-low, read once at entry. Full history, all four pairs, one shared cost model (1.6-pip spread, entry side only).

Read this before the dollar figures below

This mechanism trades ~386 times a day combined across four pairs — 141,000+ times a year. The equity curve and Monte Carlo further down reuse this project's standard account model (risk 5% of equity per trade, capped at 5% of $2,000,000, trades risked one after another in exit-time order) because that's the established, requested methodology — but that model assumes you can sequentially re-risk 5% of the account hundreds of times a day, which no real account or broker allows, and ignores that many of these trades are open on multiple pairs at the same time, not one after another. At this frequency, the model isn't wrong about the trade stream — it's wrong about the sizing. Read the win rate, pips, and profit-factor numbers as the real signal; read the dollar and "years to $1M" numbers as a mechanical stress-test of the account model, not a forecast.

(A) stays profitable at every threshold and generally gets stronger as tiny zones are excluded — the 150-pip dip is just 215 trades, too few to read as a real reversal. This confirms the reward:risk story from the discussion that led here — it just needed the cost-dominated tiny zones excluded to show up net of spread. 5-10 pips looks like the best-supported floor for either variant: past the small-zone cost trap, while 10 pips alone still leaves 223k+ trades per variant — a real sample, not a curiosity.

What this is (and isn't)

The trade list is the trustworthy layer. 3.3M+ trades per variant, full history (2003/2009/2018 → 2026 depending on the pair), the exact overshoot-then-tag entry gate and cost model from dtfx.md. Win rate, avg pips, and profit factor above are computed directly from that trade list and don't depend on any position-sizing assumption.
The dollar figures depend entirely on the account model, and that model (5% sequential risk, no concurrency limit) was not built for a strategy trading 386 times a day — it was inherited from this project's lower-frequency strategies, reused here on request rather than redesigned. A meaningful $ backtest at this frequency would need real concurrency limits (this project's own two-account, margin-checked simulator, e.g. goal79_two_account_portfolio.py) and almost certainly a much smaller per-trade risk fraction.
