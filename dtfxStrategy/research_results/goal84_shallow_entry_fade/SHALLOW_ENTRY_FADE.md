## 1. Strategy mechanics

**Zone detection.** A dynamic zigzag with no fixed lookback window and no retracement
threshold: a candidate swing high/low confirms the instant a bar fails to extend it. This
is purely causal — a pivot at bar `i` is only known once bar `i`'s full range has printed.

**Zone classification.** Only *pullback* legs (retracements against the still-open trend)
become live, tradeable zones. Advancing legs are structure only — until the trend
inverts, at which point the dormant advancing leg becomes the new trend's first zone
("flip" zone). Zone boundaries are always two consecutive confirmed pivots (`top`,
`bottom`).

**Entry — shallow retracement.** Rather than waiting for price to return to the zone's
50% midpoint (the original "v1" version of this strategy), entry is placed at **30% of
the zone's height from the near edge** — a shallower retracement that fills more often
and, once the entry-price bug below was fixed, still carries a real edge.

**Inversion.** The position taken is the *opposite* side of the zone's own implied
direction — i.e., this fades the zone's natural "reversion" signal rather than trading
it. Stop and target are the zone's far/near boundaries for the side actually traded.

**Costs.** 1.6-pip typical spread, charged once on entry (repriced for the side actually
traded — see the bug fix below); no additional cost is charged on the exit leg (a
deliberate, disclosed simplification carried from earlier in this research line, not
unique to this report).

**Time-in-trade cap.** A touch must resolve (hit stop or target) within 1,440 one-minute
bars (24h) of zone creation, or the candidate is discarded entirely. Measured directly
against the live data: this drops only **0.02–0.11%** of all zone touches across the four
pairs — negligible.

**Execution / concurrency.** One resting order per side (buy / sell), **globally across
all four pairs** — matching `PRODLIVETRADING/bot.py`'s real production constraint, not an
unlimited-concurrency approximation.

**Position sizing.** Two separate $100 sub-accounts (buy-only, sell-only — never pooled),
matching this project's real dual-OANDA-account live architecture. Risk per trade = 5% of
`min(buy_equity, sell_equity)`, capped at 50x leverage, with a $2,000,000 position-sizing
cap (see §4, Limitations).

## 2. Correctness history

An earlier version of this strategy's resolver (`goal77`–`goal81`) repriced the entry's
spread cost using the zone's own *un-traded* approach direction instead of the side
*actually* being traded once inverted — this artificially shrank the losing side's risk
distance and grew the winning side's reward distance simultaneously, nearly doubling the
reward:risk ratio as a pure artifact. Fixed in
[`goal82_audit_inversion_entry_bug.py`](../../research_scripts/goal82_audit_inversion_entry_bug.py),
verified with a worked numerical example and by rerunning the full 17-year, 4-pair
backtest with the one-line fix.

This session additionally re-audited, from scratch, every other place a bug of this kind
could hide, specifically because this exact bug had already been found once:
- **Lookahead** in zone/pivot confirmation and the touch-search loop — confirmed causal
  (a pivot / touch is never used before the bar that revealed it).
- **Fill-bar ambiguity** (both stop and target reachable within the same bar as the entry
  touch) — worked through the zone geometry by hand: for this specific inverted-fade
  structure, a target hit on the fill bar can only occur via a monotonic continuation of
  the same move that caused the touch (safe to credit), while a stop hit on the fill bar
  is credited unconditionally, which if anything is *overly conservative* (asymmetric bias
  against the strategy, not for it).
- **Silently dropped unresolved candidates** — measured directly (see above): negligible.
- **Position sizing / margin arithmetic** — matches `PRODLIVETRADING`'s live sizing exactly;
  no arithmetic bug found.
- **Concurrency bookkeeping** (open-slot tracking in the two-account event queue) —
  increments/decrements symmetrically, settles past exits before evaluating new entries.

A live re-run during that audit reproduced this report's exact headline numbers
(PF 1.366, WR 46.64%), confirming nothing had drifted.

## 5. Limitations (read before trusting a dollar figure)

- **The $2,000,000 position-sizing cap does not cap total equity growth.** It only stops
  per-trade dollar risk from scaling once `min(buy_equity, sell_equity)` exceeds $2M —
  equity itself keeps compounding without bound above that point. With ~7,275 trades/year
  and positive expectancy, this produces mathematically correct but practically
  meaningless totals (the full run ends at $1.5B combined; some individual years in the
  fresh-$200 sweep exceed $100M). The **trade-level statistics above the fold — win rate,
  R:R, profit factor, monthly/yearly/thirds consistency — do not depend on account size and
  are not affected by this.** Only the raw dollar figures and the daily-equity-derived
  Sharpe/skew/kurtosis are.
- **No cost on the exit leg.** A stop-out is modeled as landing exactly on the stop price
  with no additional spread/slippage; a real fill would likely be worse by some fraction of
  a pip, which would modestly reduce the reported profit factor.
- **No live execution validation.** This is a historical bar-replay backtest against
  1-minute LSE-vendor OHLC bars, not a live or paper-traded result. Real fills, latency,
  and broker-specific spread widening during news are not modeled beyond the fixed
  1.6-pip assumption.
- **Excess kurtosis of 112** on daily equity returns is a direct symptom of the sizing-cap
  effect above (a handful of days where equity crosses a compounding threshold dominate
  the tail) — it should not be read as "this strategy has fat-tailed blow-up risk"; the
  per-trade R-multiple distribution (bounded at −1R, excess kurtosis −1.70) is the more
  honest description of single-trade risk.
