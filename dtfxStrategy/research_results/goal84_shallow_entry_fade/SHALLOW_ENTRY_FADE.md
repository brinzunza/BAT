# Shallow-Entry Fade — 30% Retracement, Inverted

**Full backtest report, 2009-09-25 → 2026-09-03 (17.0 years), EURUSD / GBPUSD / USDJPY / EURGBP, 1-minute bars.**

> **Correction #2 (2026-09-23):** an independent review found a floating-point bug in
> `goal79_two_account_portfolio.py::simulate_two_account`'s margin-acceptance check
> (`if margin <= avail`, no tolerance). Position sizing is frequently leverage-capped by
> construction, which computes `margin` via a divide→multiply→divide round-trip through
> `max_leverage` that lands it within ~1e-14 of `avail` — sometimes a hair *above* it purely
> from floating-point rounding. The unguarded `<=` spuriously rejected fully affordable
> trades whenever that happened. Fixed by adding `tolerance = 1e-9 * max(1, abs(avail))` to
> both `simulate_two_account` and `run_monte_carlo_two_account`. **Accepted trades rise from
> 120,170 to 146,301 (+21.7%); win rate and profit factor are essentially unchanged (PF
> 1.188→1.194) since the added trades are statistically similar to the ones that already
> passed — but max drawdown worsens (−11.5%→−20.2%) and dollar totals shift accordingly.**
> All numbers below are post-fix. See §2 and §5.
>
> **Correction #1 (2026-09-23):** the first version of this report priced spread by
> repricing `entry_price` up to one spread depending on whether the touch bar was "deep"
> enough, then defined "1R" off that shifted entry — asymmetric, since a stop-out is
> tautologically `-1.0 * eff_risk` regardless of entry repricing, so losing trades never

## 1. Strategy mechanics

**Zone detection.** A dynamic zigzag with no fixed lookback window and no retracement
threshold: a candidate swing high/low confirms the instant a bar fails to extend it. This
is purely causal — a pivot at bar `i` is only known once bar `i`'s full range has printed.

**Zone classification.** Only *pullback* legs (retracements against the still-open trend)
become live, tradeable zones. Advancing legs are structure only — until the trend
inverts, at which point the dormant advancing leg becomes the new trend's first zone
("flip" zone). Zone boundaries are always two consecutive confirmed pivots (`top`,
`bottom`).

**Entry — shallow retracement.** Rather than waiting for price to return to the zone's
50% midpoint (the original "v1" version of this strategy), entry is placed at **30% of
the zone's height from the near edge** — a shallower retracement that fills more often
and, once the entry-price bug below was fixed, still carries a real edge.

**Inversion.** The position taken is the *opposite* side of the zone's own implied
direction — i.e., this fades the zone's natural "reversion" signal rather than trading
it. Stop and target are the zone's far/near boundaries for the side actually traded.

**Costs.** 1.6-pip typical spread. Entry, stop, and target are all held at the exact raw
zone/technical levels — no price is ever repriced — and exactly **one spread's worth of
R-cost is subtracted from every trade's raw result, whether it won or lost** (§2 explains
why this replaced an earlier, asymmetric convention). No additional cost is charged
beyond that single spread.

**Time-in-trade cap.** A touch must resolve (hit stop or target) within 1,440 one-minute
bars (24h) of zone creation, or the candidate is discarded entirely. Measured directly
against the live data: this drops only **0.02–0.11%** of all zone touches across the four
pairs — negligible.

**Execution / concurrency.** One resting order per side (buy / sell), **globally across
all four pairs** — matching `PRODLIVETRADING/bot.py`'s real production constraint, not an
unlimited-concurrency approximation.

**Position sizing.** Two separate $100 sub-accounts (buy-only, sell-only — never pooled),
matching this project's real dual-OANDA-account live architecture. Risk per trade = 5% of
`min(buy_equity, sell_equity)`, capped at 50x leverage, with a $2,000,000 position-sizing
cap (see §5, Limitations).

## 2. Correctness history

**Entry-price bug (fixed in `goal82`).** An earlier resolver (`goal77`–`goal81`) repriced
the entry's spread cost using the zone's own *un-traded* approach direction instead of the
side *actually* being traded once inverted. Fixed by computing spread-shift direction from
the actually-traded side.

**Spread-cost convention (fixed in `goal85`).** The `goal82` fix still repriced
`entry_price` itself, folding cost into the definition of "1R" and structurally exempting
losing trades from ever paying it. `goal85` removes the repricing entirely: entry/stop/
target are the raw zone levels, and `spread_price / eff_risk` (exactly one spread, in R
units) is subtracted from every trade's raw outcome, win or loss.

**Margin-acceptance floating-point bug (fixed in `goal79`, this correction).** Found via
an independent cross-check: another implementation of this exact spread/entry convention
(built separately, for a 2026 walk-forward analysis) produced a 36x larger ending balance
than this report on an *identical* trade set at 5% fixed risk. Tracing the two simulators
in lockstep isolated the divergence to a single comparison: `if margin <= avail`, with no
floating-point tolerance. Because position sizing is frequently leverage-capped (`margin`
derived via `risk_base*leverage/entry_price*entry_price/leverage`, a round trip that
doesn't always land back on exactly the same float as `avail = risk_base`), the unguarded
`<=` spuriously rejected fully-affordable trades whenever rounding pushed `margin`
infinitesimally (~1e-14) above `avail`. This was a common case, not a rare edge case,
because the model is designed to size positions right up to the leverage ceiling. Fixed by
adding a `1e-9`-relative tolerance, matching the independent implementation that surfaced
the bug. Confirmed the fix doesn't change the qualitative picture: win rate and profit
factor are essentially unchanged; only trade count, drawdown, and dollar totals move.

This session additionally re-audited every other place a bug of this kind could hide:
- **Lookahead** in zone/pivot confirmation and the touch-search loop — confirmed causal.
- **Fill-bar ambiguity** (stop and target both reachable within the touch bar) — a target
  hit on the fill bar can only occur via a monotonic continuation of the same move that
  caused the touch (safe to credit); a stop hit on the fill bar is credited
  unconditionally, which if anything is *overly conservative*.
- **Silently dropped unresolved candidates** — measured directly: negligible (0.02–0.11%).
- **Position sizing / margin arithmetic** (the formula itself, not the comparison bug
  above) — matches `PRODLIVETRADING`'s live sizing exactly, cross-validated against an
  independently-written implementation (`quant_engine.sizing.size_and_margin`) trade-for-
  trade.
- **Concurrency bookkeeping** — increments/decrements symmetrically, settles past exits
  before evaluating new entries.

## 5. Limitations (read before trusting a dollar figure)

- **The $2,000,000 position-sizing cap does not cap total equity growth.** It only stops
  per-trade dollar risk from scaling once `min(buy_equity, sell_equity)` exceeds $2M —
  equity itself keeps compounding without bound above that point. With ~8,630 trades/year
  and positive expectancy, this produces mathematically correct but practically
  meaningless totals (the full run ends at $983M combined). The **trade-level statistics
  above the fold — win rate, R:R, profit factor, monthly/yearly/thirds consistency — do
  not depend on account size and are not affected by this.** Only the raw dollar figures
  and the daily-equity-derived Sharpe/skew/kurtosis are.
- **No live execution validation.** This is a historical bar-replay backtest against
  1-minute LSE-vendor OHLC bars, not a live or paper-traded result.
- **Excess kurtosis of ~190** on daily equity returns is a direct symptom of the
  sizing-cap effect above, now more pronounced than before the margin-bug fix (more
  accepted trades compound to a higher, still-capped-per-trade equity level faster) — not
  evidence of a fat-tailed edge. The per-trade R-multiple distribution (bounded, excess
  kurtosis −1.97) is the more honest description of single-trade risk.
- **The edge is real but thin, and now shown with a materially deeper realistic
  drawdown.** PF 1.194, 3 of 204 months negative, but a −20.24% max drawdown (vs. the
  −11.48% this report showed before the margin-bug fix) is a meaningfully different risk
  picture for anyone sizing a real allocation off this number. The strategy still clears
  100% of years and all three independent thirds of history individually.
- **Every correction so far has moved trade count, drawdown, and dollar totals — but not
  the core win rate / profit factor.** That consistency across three independent fixes
  (entry-price direction, spread convention, margin-comparison epsilon) is itself evidence
  the ~1.2 PF, ~46.5% WR edge is a real property of the signal, not an artifact of any one
  of these bugs — but it also means every dollar/drawdown figure in this document should be
  read as "as accurate as this codebase's current state," not as a settled final answer.
